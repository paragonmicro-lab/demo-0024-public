    Student Guide¶

    Version 0.9.2 · draft · 2026-10-14 · class/student-guide.md

    What you will learn¶

    You take a real Windows Server + PostgreSQL VM from VMware vSphere onto Red Hat OpenShift Virtualization, cold and then warm. You move it between two OpenShift clusters and protect it with OpenShift Data Foundation disaster recovery. At every step you write your own data into the VM's database and prove that none of it was lost, changed or reordered.

    This guide is the map. The steps are in the demo guides below.

    Your login, and what you need¶

    The instructor gives you your login. One student username and one password open everything in this class:

    Resource Sign in as
    vSphere Client (vCenter) and both OpenShift consoles your class ID, with SSO (Entra)
    Your Windows VM .\student-NN
    Your VM's PostgreSQL database student_NN

    All you need is a web browser. Nothing is installed on your computer. You work in the vSphere Client, the two OpenShift consoles, and your browser terminal: a shell in your own pod in the OpenShift console, where every command in these guides runs. Copy a command here with its copy button; paste it there with right-click → Paste.

    Lab 0 walks you through it in about 5 minutes: open the tabs, sign in to vCenter, both OpenShift clusters and PostgreSQL, and see your VM and your access.

    💡 A page stuck after a timeout? Sign in again in a private window. Nothing is lost: your VM, your data and your terminal live in the lab, not in the browser.

    How the lab is put together¶

    Each class gets its own three environments (lab diagram):

    Environment What it is You
    vSphere vCenter + ESXi, where your VM starts inspect your VM, enable CBT, shut it down
    Site A OpenShift + OpenShift Virtualization + MTV, on ODF storage migrate your VM in, run it, snapshot it
    Site B the same on NFS storage, plus the ACM / DR hub move your VM in, fail over to it

    The zone names (lz1, ocp-lz1, …) change per booking; the guides use them as examples. Your browser terminal already knows your own (type ovedr).

    • Your own VM, win-pg-s<NN>: a full copy of the instructor's, with its own address, in your own vCenter folder. You power it, snapshot it, migrate it and fail it over yourself.
    • Your own namespace on both clusters, named your class ID, where you are admin. Your VM lands there, and your browser terminal lives there.
    • Everything is staged; you do the last step. Each demo's pieces (operators, maps, a migration plan for your VM) are pre-built, and each guide shows how to build them yourself (Lab S). Your task is to review and start.

    The demos, in order¶

    Every stage follows one rhythm: inject your own data → change something → verify it all arrived intact.

    Core: the migration class. Everyone does these, in order.

    # Guide You will Time Bonus (optional)
    0 Access and tools sign in to vCenter, both clusters and PostgreSQL; open a command line 5 min —
    1 Cold migration check your plan and the vCenter link; shut down, migrate, review and power on in OpenShift; inject and read back your rows 45 min 1a: snapshot and restore (25 min) · 1b: live and storage migration (20 min) · 1c: cross-cluster migration (45 min) · 1d: a cloud-native golden image (30 min)
    2 Warm migration with storage copy offload 🚧 coming soon (read only): needs a SAN array this lab doesn't have yet. Why the lab dropped VDDK; the array copies the disks while the VM runs; cut over; snapshot on the array — —

    Go deeper, when your instructor offers it:

    Group Guide You will Time Bonus (optional)
    Storage Demo 4: ODF Regional-DR replicate every 15 minutes, fail over, reverse, come home clean 90 min §4.8 hostile failover (30 min)
    Backup Demo 5: OADP backup and restore back up your namespace, delete your VM, restore it, prove the data 30 min framework: when OADP is staged

    Reference: Toolkit (your terminal and the data commands) · Lab S (how every staged piece was built, with the commands and the Red Hat docs) · Lab diagram.

    Times are estimates until the class has been timed. Every demo ends the same way: you read your rows back, and ovedr_verify shows chain_ok = t. That read-back is your evidence that nothing was lost.

    Getting help: a step marked [verify] is still being proven in the lab. If one fails, tell the instructor rather than improvising. Each section has a 🎬 recording of the same steps in the console.
